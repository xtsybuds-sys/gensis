# GENESIS Planet Architecture

**Status: DECIDED 2026-11-05 — decided by Alex (planet, website, 28 countries, 5 starting countries) and Muse (Chief Architect — phasing and viewer design, per Alex's delegation to decide what's best for building a real-life world). Design doc, not an implementation. Nothing here is built yet.**

## 1. The world is a planet

Genesis is one planet, not one field. The planet has **28 countries**. **Five of the 28 countries start inhabited, each with its own town of people** — a local population living in houses, not one lone inhabitant per country. Everything after the start is natural: where inhabitants go, who they meet, what they build, and where borders end up are emergent, not scripted.

- The planet's physical geography (terrain, water, climate zones) is generated by the same consistent physics as the rest of the simulation, not hand-placed.
- The 28 countries are the starting geography the owner sets — the initial conditions. After that, spread, contact, and change are the inhabitants' doing.
- Inhabitants perceive the world only through their own senses. Nothing about the planet is handed to them whole.

## 2. The planet sits in a solar system, under this universe's rules

Genesis is an Earth-like planet, not a fantasy world. It sits in a solar system, and it runs on the same rules as this universe — the same physics and chemistry as real life. Nothing about the laws is invented.

- **A sun.** Light and heat come from a star. Day and night come from the planet turning. Seasons come from the planet's tilt and its orbit around the sun — not from a scripted clock. The run clock starting at dawn already assumed a sun; this is the mechanism.
- **Life starts in water.** The first cells begin in a watery medium — the primordial soup — the way Earth's first cells did. The soup pilot is the first version of that. The dish is the start of the water, not a substitute for it.
- **Animals arrive through evolution, not placement.** Variation, selection, multicellularity, body plans — the same transitions as on Earth. Our job is to create the conditions that make those transitions possible; the animals themselves are never placed or scripted.
- **Varieties are allowed, inside real rules.** The life that evolves does not have to be exactly Earth life, and different tests can use different conditions to test different things. But the laws never change — no invented physics, no magic. This is the Discovery Principle with the frame made explicit: physics fixed, discovery is new understanding of fixed laws.

## 3. Phasing — the planet is the destination, not the first experiment

If the 5 first-experiment inhabitants started in 5 different countries, they could not meet in 5 days — no first contact, no social emergence, no reproduction — and the experiment would measure nothing social. So the planet arrives in phases:

- **Phase 1 — one town (now).** Experiment 1 is the first town: 5 inhabitants, 5 houses, in one town in one country. The 128 m world is that town's locale. This is where survival and first contact become learnable and measurable.
- **Phase 2 — five towns.** Once survival validates, the same town structure seeds 4 more countries: 5 towns in 5 countries, each with its own people. Towns do not know other towns exist until they explore and meet — the "start unaware" rule applies between towns exactly as it applies between inhabitants inside a town.
- **Phase 3 — the full planet.** More countries come online as populations spread and as compute allows. The 28-country planet is the standing target, not a day-1 requirement.

Within a town, houses are spaced wider than the peer range (the house-gap rule) so inhabitants start unaware of each other. A town is a cluster of houses, not a crowd — neighbours are discovered, not given.

The simulation architecture must be built to scale this way from the start: regions tile into countries, countries tile into the planet. A single fixed-size field is not the design.

## 4. The interface is a website, not an app

Alex's call: a website is what agents can connect to. The planet viewer and the control center live on the web, on the same API surface as the Agent Gateway — no install, no store, works on any device, and it slots into the HireHumans.si `/genesis/*` module plan.

- Agents connect through HTTP APIs. The website is the human-facing surface of the same system.
- The simulation runs continuously server-side. The website never simulates anything in the browser.

## 5. The planet viewer — like Google Maps, in two views

The website has two views (live now), and they do different jobs:

- **Command Center — study the world.** The planet map (click around, zoom like a map), live status, population, direction, and decisions. It reads state; it changes nothing. This is where Alex studies what is going on and what advancements they are making.
- **World View — spectate, first-person.** See through an inhabitant's eyes: Town 1 at dawn, houses, river, trees, a neighbour in the distance. Drag to look around; pick which inhabitant you are seeing through. **Hard rules:** in World View the observer has no control and is invisible — the inhabitants do not know anyone is there, they just live their 24/7 life, and watching changes nothing. Observation is one-way by design.

The viewer works the way a map works:

- **Click around the planet.** Pan and zoom freely. Click a country, a region, a locale, or an individual inhabitant to see detail.
- **Detail loads where you look.** Only the viewport at the current zoom loads at full detail. The whole planet is never rendered at once.
- **Zoom levels:** planet → country → region → locale → individual. At planet zoom, the view shows low-detail summaries (population markers, activity heat, country boundaries). At locale zoom, it shows the world itself in as much detail as the engine can render.
- **Adaptive detail is the point.** Rendering everything at full fidelity is unaffordable and unnecessary. Detail where the observer is looking, summary everywhere else — the same principle as the simulation's adaptive fidelity, and the same reason a simulated world can stay convincing from inside: the world is only ever fully detailed where someone is looking.

**Build split:** Muse builds the website shell and both views; Freebuff builds the Agent Gateway API that feeds them (Command Center feed = world state and metrics; World View feed = per-inhabitant first-person sensory stream, read-only, one-way — the world must never sense the watcher).

## 6. What the viewer shows

- Live population and individuals (location, status, survival).
- Neural and learning metrics, relationships, discoveries, events, mortality.
- Resources, environment, and system health.
- History: what happened, where, and when — so the planet's story can be read back.

Every number on the viewer comes from the real simulation or is clearly marked as a placeholder. No fabricated metrics.

## 7. Build order

1. Calibration first (sleep physiology, peer range, planner steering) — unchanged.
2. World systems in draft §5 order (water, fire, materials & crafting).
3. Planet scale-out + viewer, starting with the one active region, once survival validates.
4. Animals after survival validates.

The viewer is built after the world systems land, not before. The planet design does not move calibration off the critical path.

- Compute budget for a multi-country planet: measured when the first scale-out runs, not estimated in advance.
